Guided Discovery Questions

Run the simulation to gather evidence and answer the following questions on your lab report using the Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning (CER) framework.

1. CER — Starting Position (y-intercept)

Question: What physical quantity does the y-intercept of the position-vs-time graph represent for each car?

  • Claim: State clearly what physical quantity matches the y-intercept.
  • Evidence: Select your group's scenario. Run Trial A (Red) and Trial B (Blue). Observe the starting coordinate of each car at time t = 0 s and note their values.
  • Reasoning: Explain why the initial starting position x0 and the mathematical y-intercept must be equal in an accurate model of constant velocity motion.
2. CER — Direction of Motion (slope sign)

Question: What does the sign (+ or −) of the slope tell you about the car's motion?

  • Claim: Answer the question directly in one clear sentence.
  • Evidence: Contrast Red Car A (moving right) and Blue Car B (moving left). What is the sign of the slope/velocity for each? What happens to their positions as time increases?
  • Reasoning: Explain why motion toward increasing positions gives a positive slope (Δx > 0), whereas motion toward decreasing positions gives a negative slope (Δx < 0).
3. Interpreting Slope and Y-Intercept Mathematically

Task: Fill in the blanks to interpret the slope and y-intercept of your motion graphs.

  1. Every time the __________ (independent variable) increases by 1 second, the __________ (dependent variable) changes by __________ (value and unit).
  2. The y-intercept represents the value of __________ (dependent variable) when the __________ (independent variable) is 0 seconds.

Example template: "Every time the time increases by 1 second, the position of Red Car A increases by 30.0 cm."

4. CER — Why Slope Represents Velocity

Question: State what physical quantity the slope of a position-vs-time graph represents, and explain why.

  • Claim: Identify the physical quantity represented by the slope.
  • Evidence: Write the mathematical definition of slope:
    slope = ΔyΔx = y2 − y1x2 − x1
    Then write the physics definition of average velocity:
    vavg = ΔxΔt = xf − xitf − ti
    Compare the numerator and denominator units.
  • Reasoning: Explain how matching the numerator (change in position) and denominator (change in time) connects the math concept of slope to the physics definition of velocity.
5. CER — Experimental Uncertainty & Line-of-Best-Fit Analysis

Question: When using Manual Timing mode, why do data points scatter slightly around the line of best fit rather than falling on an exact line?

  • Claim: State the primary source of experimental uncertainty in manual stopwatch data collection.
  • Evidence: Compare your manual stopwatch split times with the auto photogate times. Cite the coefficient of determination (R2) from your line fit.
  • Reasoning: Explain how reaction time latency introduces random experimental error, and explain why drawing a line of best fit (linear regression) is superior to connecting the dots because it averages out random scatter.
6. Limits of the Constant Velocity Particle Model

Question: Our mathematical model assumes the cars move at exactly constant velocity forever. Give one specific physical reason this assumption breaks down in a real laboratory, and describe what the position-vs-time graph would look like if data collection continued.

Consider battery depletion, motor warming, track friction variations, or hitting the end stops. How would the curve shape change?

Theory & Modeling Reference

The Constant Velocity Particle Model (CVPM)

When an object covers equal displacements in equal intervals of time, it moves with constant velocity.

x(t) = v · t + x0
  • x: Position at time t (cm) — Dependent variable
  • t: Elapsed time (s) — Independent variable
  • v: Constant velocity (cm/s) — Slope of the graph
  • x0: Initial position at t = 0 (cm) — y-intercept

Dealing with Experimental Uncertainty

In real physics experiments, measurements have uncertainty due to:

  • Reaction Timing Error: Delay in observing a car cross a tape mark and clicking split.
  • Parallax Error: Viewing the car bumper and ruler from an angle.
  • Line of Best Fit: Rather than connecting data points dot-to-dot, linear regression balances data above and below the line, canceling out random timing errors to find the true underlying velocity.
